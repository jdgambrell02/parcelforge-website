The status quo

Title defect work is still done by hand — usually with a legal pad.

Before any home, farm, or commercial property can be bought, sold, or financed, someone has to prove the owner owns it free and clear: no hidden liens, no missing heirs, no tax sale buried in a county record from 30 years ago.

In most of the country that's done entirely by hand. An attorney or abstractor drives to the courthouse, pulls physical deed books, searches handwritten indexes 40–60 years back, and pieces the chain together one document at a time. When they find a problem — and they usually do — they draft the cure from scratch. In Mississippi alone, more than 1,500 real estate attorneys do this across 82 counties.

Find any property

Search by parcel ID, PPIN, address, or owner name. AtlasVA confirms ownership, legal description, assessed value, and last sale before it begins.

County-aware retrieval

It knows the right records source for each of the 82 counties and pulls the recorded instruments automatically — no courthouse trip, no manual index searching.

Reads every instrument

AtlasVA captures each record image and TitleCure AI reads it into structured data — recording and execution dates, book/page, grantor and grantee, grantor capacity, spousal joinder, consideration, and legal description.

Classifies and builds the chain

Every instrument is normalized to a standard type, then assembled into the grantor-to-grantee chain, oldest to most recent, with gaps flagged along the way.

Parcel-lineage reconstruction

It detects parcel creation anchors and resolves subdivision parent-tracts, retrieving upstream instruments for a lineage-complete abstract — not a search-window-limited list.

Feeds the assessment modules

The structured chain auto-answers a large share of the statutory assessment questions, so examiners spend their time on judgment calls — not data entry.

A previously seen instrument is served from AtlasVA's index cache instantly — so parcels in the same subdivision or chain reuse work already done.

Where this goes

Title search becomes a database query.

In Texas and California, title companies keep private title plants — databases of every recorded instrument, so a search is instant. Mississippi and most abstract states have nothing like it; every search starts cold. ParcelForge is building the public-access version: a continuously updated knowledge graph where every parcel, deed, mortgage, lien, tax sale, and release is a node — and the relationships between them are queryable.
